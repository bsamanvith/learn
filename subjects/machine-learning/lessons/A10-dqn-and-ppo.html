Deep RL from scratch: DQN on CartPole, PPO on Pendulum

Build the two workhorses of deep reinforcement learning in NumPy, with no autograd and no RL library. First DQN: a replay buffer, Double DQN targets, the Huber loss and a Polyak-averaged target network, trained on CartPole. Then PPO for continuous actions: a Gaussian policy and its score function, generalised advantage estimation across episode boundaries, the clipped objective and its gradient, trained on the pendulum swing-up. Every piece is checked against finite differences or a brute-force computation before you train with it. Expect six to ten hours.

Set-up

You need Python 3 and NumPy. From the repository root:

cd subjects/machine-learning/assignments/A10-dqn-and-ppo
cp starter.py mine.py          # work in your own copy
python3 check.py mine.py       # run as often as you like (under a minute once everything works)

Given: CartPole (lesson 37's, with scaled observations) and Pendulum (the swing-up of Gym's Pendulum-v1: torque in $[-2, 2]$, reward $-(\theta^2 + 0.1\dot\theta^2 + 0.001u^2)$, 200 steps per episode, so a random policy scores about $-1{,}200$ and a good one about $-150$ to $-200$); an MLP whose backward takes $\partial L/\partial\text{output}$, stores the parameter gradients and returns $\partial L/\partial\text{input}$; Adam with optional gradient-norm clipping; and GaussianPolicy, an MLP for the mean with a state-independent log standard deviation. Both environments return (observation, reward, terminated, truncated): the distinction matters, because a truncated episode must bootstrap from the next state's value and a terminated one must not.

Experiments and write-up

Once all ten checks pass, run the experiments below. The solution's numbers are quoted for reference; DQN in particular varies a lot between seeds, so compare shapes and orders of magnitude, not decimals.

  1. DQN and its instability. Train train_dqn for 100,000 steps on seeds 0, 1 and 2, plot the return per episode (smoothed) against steps, and evaluate the final greedy policy on 20 episodes. In the solution, the mean return per 20,000-step window rises from about 60 to between 110 and 235 by the end, and the greedy policies score 206, 500 and 500. Training curves are far noisier than the greedy scores. Explain why the return during training understates the greedy policy (what does $\varepsilon = 0.05$ cost on CartPole?), and plot the largest $|Q|$ in each batch to see whether the values stay near the true $\sum_t\gamma^t\le100$.
  2. Double DQN, ablated. Repeat with double=False. In the solution the plain version's greedy policies score 132, 89 and 245, but its training curves are not clearly worse. Measure the overestimation directly instead: for states from the buffer, compare the network's $\max_aQ(s, a)$ with the discounted return actually obtained from $s$ by the greedy policy. Is the bias larger without the double estimator?
  3. PPO on the pendulum. Train train_ppo with the defaults ($\gamma = 0.9$, $\lambda = 0.95$, 2,048 steps per batch, 10 epochs of minibatches of 64, $\varepsilon = 0.2$, learning rate $10^{-3}$) for 300,000 steps on three seeds, and evaluate the deterministic policy (the mean action) on 20 episodes. In the solution, the mean return per 50,000-step window goes from about $-900$ in the first to $-230$ in the second and $-190$ to $-220$ in the third, the deterministic policies score $-209$, $-185$ and $-207$, and the standard deviation of the policy shrinks from 0.61 to about 0.2. Watch a few episodes and describe the swing-up strategy it found.
  4. What each ingredient does. Repeat experiment 3 with $\gamma = 0.99$, with one epoch per batch, and with no clipping ($\varepsilon = 10^9$). The solution's last-50,000-step returns: $\gamma = 0.99$ gives $-211$, $-270$, $-183$ (it learns, but takes about twice as many steps to get there); one epoch gives $-339$, $-387$, $-965$ after 300,000 steps (it is still learning: each sample is used once); no clipping gives $-1{,}434$, $-1{,}559$, $-1{,}309$, worse than random, with the policy's standard deviation collapsed to 0.012 to 0.014. Explain the last result: what do ten unclipped epochs on one batch do to $\log\sigma$, and why can the agent not recover once $\sigma$ is that small?
  5. Your choice. Do one of: (a) add the entropy bonus $c\,\mathbb{E}[H(\pi)]$ to PPO, derive its gradient for the Gaussian (it depends only on $\log\sigma$) and test whether it prevents the collapse of experiment 4; (b) implement a dueling network head for DQN and compare; (c) run PPO with a categorical policy on CartPole and compare its sample efficiency with your DQN.
