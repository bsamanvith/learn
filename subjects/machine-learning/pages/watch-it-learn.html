Machine learning · Lessons 01 to 03

Watch it learn

Three short animations of the same idea: a model starts wrong, measures how wrong it is, and nudges its knobs downhill. Nothing here is faked. Every frame is a real gradient descent step on real data.

Animation 1

A line finding its place

The orange line starts tilted the wrong way. The thin orange ticks are the errors, one per point. Each step moves the slope w and the intercept b against the gradient, the errors shrink, and the loss on the right falls. The faint lines are the last few steps.

Learning rate

Try 0.05: the same walk, just slower, and it runs out of steps before it lands. Try 0.9: the intercept overshoots back and forth before it settles.

Four learning rates, one bowl

bowl curvature c = 2·mean(x²) · stable only while η < 2 / c

Every hop multiplies the distance to the bottom by (1 − η·c). Below 1 in size, the ball settles. Between 1 and 2, it overshoots but each jump is smaller, so it zig-zags in. Past 2, each jump is bigger than the last.

Animation 3

Logistic regression: bending a line into a probability

Students who passed sit on the top row, those who failed on the bottom. The model starts as a flat 0.5 ("no idea"). Gradient descent on log-loss tilts and slides the S-curve until it rises where passes begin. The dashed line is the decision boundary, where the model says 50/50. The thin lines are each student's error, p − y: the same "delta" as in linear regression.

The curve never becomes a perfect step, because a few students near the middle went the "wrong" way. Log-loss would rather be honestly unsure about them than confidently wrong.
