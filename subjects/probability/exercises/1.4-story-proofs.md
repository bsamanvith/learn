# Exercises 1.4 — Story proofs

🟢 warm-up · 🟡 core · 🔴 stretch · 🟣 proof · 🔁 callback · 💻 simulate. Solutions at the bottom.
For every "prove", give a **story**. Algebra is allowed only where it says so.

---

### 1.4.1 🟢 Row 7
Write row 7 of Pascal's triangle ($\binom70, \dots, \binom77$) using Pascal's rule on row 6. Check that it sums to $2^7$ and that its alternating sum is 0.

### 1.4.2 🟢 Coefficients
Find **(a)** the coefficient of $x^3y^5$ in $(x+y)^8$, **(b)** the coefficient of $x^2$ in $(1 + 2x)^5$, **(c)** the coefficient of $a^2bc$ in $(a + b + c)^4$.

### 1.4.3 🟢 Routes through a point
On a grid, how many right/up routes go from $(0,0)$ to $(6,4)$? How many pass through $(3,2)$? How many **avoid** $(3,2)$?

### 1.4.4 🟢 Handshakes and triangles
$n$ points lie on a circle. How many chords join pairs of them? How many triangles have their corners among the points? Give the story for each.

### 1.4.5 🟡 Committees with a twist
Prove $\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j} = \binom{n}{j}\binom{n-j}{k-j}$. *(Hint: a committee of $k$, with a subcommittee of $j$ inside it.)*

### 1.4.6 🟡 Average number of heads
**(a)** Prove $\sum_{k=0}^n k\binom nk = n2^{n-1}$. (Story: committees of any size with a chair.)
**(b)** Deduce that the average number of heads, over all $2^n$ equally likely sequences of $n$ fair flips, is $n/2$.

### 1.4.7 🟡 Squares
Prove $\sum_k \binom nk^2 = \binom{2n}{n}$ directly with lattice paths. *(Hint: routes from $(0,0)$ to $(n,n)$ crossing the anti-diagonal $x + y = n$.)*

### 1.4.8 🟡 Hockey stick, used
Use the hockey stick identity to evaluate $\binom33 + \binom43 + \binom53 + \cdots + \binom{10}{3}$. Check by computing directly.

### 1.4.9 🟡 Even and odd
Prove that for $n \ge 1$ an $n$-element set has exactly $2^{n-1}$ subsets of even size. (Use the toggling pairing, not the binomial theorem.)

### 1.4.10 🔴 $\binom{2n}{n}$ is even
Prove that $\binom{2n}{n}$ is even for every $n \ge 1$.

### 1.4.11 🔴 Powers of three
Prove $\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk 2^k = 3^n$. *(Hint: what three things can happen to each element?)*

### 1.4.12 🟣 Binomial theorem
Write out the story proof of $(x+y)^n = \sum_k \binom nk x^k y^{n-k}$ carefully, then derive $\sum_k \binom nk = 2^n$ and $\sum_k (-1)^k\binom nk = 0$.

### 1.4.13 🟣 Vandermonde by algebra
Prove Vandermonde's identity by comparing the coefficient of $x^k$ on both sides of $(1+x)^{m+n} = (1+x)^m(1+x)^n$.

### 1.4.14 🔁 Committees with chairs, again *(callback to 1.3)*
In 1.3.3 you counted 168 chaired 3-person committees from 8 people in two ways. Which identity from this chapter were you proving? Check both sides numerically.

### 1.4.15 💻 Brute-force the identities
With `itertools.combinations`, enumerate subsets for $n \le 10$ and check: symmetry, Pascal's rule, the row sum, the alternating sum, the hockey stick and Vandermonde.

## Solutions

<details><summary>1.4.1</summary>

Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1. Adding neighbours gives row 7: **1, 7, 21, 35, 35, 21, 7, 1**.
Sum: $128 = 2^7$ ✓. Alternating: $1 - 7 + 21 - 35 + 35 - 21 + 7 - 1 = 0$ ✓.
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.2</summary>

**(a)** $\binom83 = 56$.
**(b)** The term is $\binom52 (2x)^2 = 10 \cdot 4x^2$, so the coefficient is **40**.
**(c)** Multinomial: $\frac{4!}{2!\,1!\,1!} = 12$. (Choose which 2 of the 4 brackets give $a$, then which remaining one gives $b$: $6 \cdot 2 = 12$.)
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.3</summary>

All routes: $\binom{10}{4} = 210$.
Through $(3,2)$: get there ($\binom52 = 10$) times finish from there (3 right and 2 up: $\binom52 = 10$), giving **100**.
Avoiding $(3,2)$: $210 - 100 = 110$. (Complement rule, in counting form.)
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.4</summary>

Chords: a chord is a 2-element set of points, so $\binom n2$.
Triangles: a triangle is a 3-element set of points, so $\binom n3$. (Points on a circle never have three in a line, so every 3 points really do make a triangle.)
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.5</summary>

Count pairs (committee $K$ of size $k$, subcommittee $J \subseteq K$ of size $j$) chosen from $n$ people.
- **Committee first:** $\binom nk$, then the subcommittee inside it: $\binom kj$.
- **Subcommittee first:** $\binom nj$, then the other $k - j$ committee members from the remaining $n - j$ people: $\binom{n-j}{k-j}$.

Both count the same pairs. ∎
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.6</summary>

**(a)** Count committees of **any size** with a chair, from $n$ people.
- By size: $\binom nk$ committees of size $k$, each with $k$ choices of chair. Total $\sum_k k\binom nk$.
- Chair first: $n$ ways. Each of the other $n - 1$ people is either on the committee or not: $2^{n-1}$. Total $n2^{n-1}$. ∎

**(b)** The number of sequences with $k$ heads is $\binom nk$. So the total number of heads, summed over all sequences, is $\sum_k k\binom nk = n2^{n-1}$.
Divide by $2^n$ sequences to get the average, $n/2$.
(Easier story: by symmetry, each flip is heads in exactly half of the sequences, so the $n$ flips contribute $n/2$ heads on average. That's linearity of expectation, Chapter 3.3.)
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.7</summary>

There are $\binom{2n}{n}$ routes from $(0,0)$ to $(n,n)$. Each crosses $x + y = n$ at exactly one point $(k, n-k)$.
Routes through that point: $\binom{n}{k}$ ways to reach it, times $\binom{n}{n-k} = \binom nk$ ways to finish (from $(k, n-k)$ to $(n,n)$ takes $n - k$ rights and $k$ ups).
Summing over $k$: $\sum_k \binom nk^2 = \binom{2n}{n}$. ∎
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.8</summary>

Hockey stick with $k = 3$ and $n = 10$: $\sum_{j=3}^{10}\binom j3 = \binom{11}{4} = 330$.
Directly: $1 + 4 + 10 + 20 + 35 + 56 + 84 + 120 = 330$ ✓.
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.9</summary>

Pair each subset $S$ with $S \triangle \{1\}$: add element 1 if it's missing, remove it if it's there. This pairing:
- is its own inverse (toggling twice gets you back), so it splits all subsets into pairs;
- changes the size by exactly 1, so each pair has one even-sized and one odd-sized subset.

So the even and odd subsets are equal in number, $2^n / 2 = 2^{n-1}$ each. ∎ (We need $n \ge 1$ so there's an element 1 to toggle.)
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.10</summary>

$\binom{2n}{n}$ counts $n$-element subsets of $\{1, \dots, 2n\}$. Pair each subset $S$ with its complement $S^c$, which also has $n$ elements.
A subset is never its own complement, since $S \cap S^c = \emptyset$ and $S$ is non-empty. So the subsets split into pairs $\{S, S^c\}$, and their number is even. ∎
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.11</summary>

$3^n$ counts the ways to label each of $n$ elements **out**, **in-red** or **in-blue**.
By cases on $k$, the number of elements that are "in" (red or blue): choose them ($\binom nk$), then colour each one red or blue ($2^k$). The rest are "out". Total $\sum_k \binom nk 2^k$. ∎

(Algebra check: the binomial theorem with $x = 2$, $y = 1$ gives $(2+1)^n$.)
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.12</summary>

In the product of $n$ brackets $(x+y)$, a term of the expansion is a choice of $x$ or $y$ from each bracket. The term equals $x^k y^{n-k}$ when exactly $k$ brackets supply $x$.
The set of those brackets is any $k$-subset of the $n$ brackets: $\binom nk$ choices. Collecting like terms gives the formula. ∎

$x = y = 1$: $2^n = \sum_k\binom nk$. $x = -1$, $y = 1$, $n \ge 1$: $0^n = 0 = \sum_k (-1)^k \binom nk$.
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.13</summary>

Left side: the coefficient of $x^k$ in $(1+x)^{m+n}$ is $\binom{m+n}{k}$.
Right side: $(1+x)^m(1+x)^n = \left(\sum_j \binom mj x^j\right)\left(\sum_i \binom ni x^i\right)$. The $x^k$ terms come from $j + i = k$, so the coefficient is $\sum_j \binom mj\binom{n}{k-j}$.
Two polynomials that are equal have equal coefficients. ∎

This is the **generating function** method. It returns big in Chapter 5.6.
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.14</summary>

The **team captain** identity, $k\binom nk = n\binom{n-1}{k-1}$, with $n = 8$ and $k = 3$: $3 \cdot 56 = 168$ and $8 \cdot 21 = 168$ ✓.
</details>

<details><summary>1.4.15</summary>

See [`sims/identities.py`](../sims/identities.py) for a full version. The core:

```python
from itertools import combinations
from math import comb
for n in range(1, 11):
    subsets = [s for k in range(n + 1) for s in combinations(range(n), k)]
    assert len(subsets) == 2 ** n
    assert sum((-1) ** len(s) for s in subsets) == 0
    for k in range(n + 1):
        assert comb(n, k) == comb(n, n - k)
        if k: assert comb(n, k) == comb(n - 1, k - 1) + comb(n - 1, k)
print("ok")
```
</details>
