The birthday problem

How many people do you need in a room before it's more likely than not that two share a birthday? Slide n and watch the curve; then fill a room and look for collisions on the calendar.

Solid line: exact P(match) = 1 − (d/d)·((d−1)/d)···((d−n+1)/d). Dashed: the approximation 1 − e−n(n−1)/(2d), which comes from counting the n(n−1)/2 pairs, each matching with chance 1/d. The 50% point is near √(2d·ln 2) ≈ 1.18√d — so it grows like the square root of the number of days.
